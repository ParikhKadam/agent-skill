When you are stuck or unsure

Most planning time goes to not knowing what to ask or what to do when an answer is missing. The skill carries that load: it picks the next question and has a move ready for each kind of uncertainty.

Deciding what to ask next

Nobody can name the question that matters while they do not yet know what they do not know. The skill uses six steps instead of asking you to define requirements.

  1. Scan the state. What is unknown, what the current layer's check still lacks, what contradicts what, and what you said that has no home yet.
  2. Rank by value of information. Prefer the unknown whose answer would change the most downstream, that is late and costly to find, and that it cannot look up itself.
  3. Look it up first. Code, docs, tickets, history and real data are its job. You are asked only for decisions, preferences, and facts only you hold.
  4. Generate from a probe if nothing comes to mind (below).
  5. Shape the question: specific to your project, one idea, with a recommended answer you can accept with "yes", and a line on what the answer would change.
  6. Know when to stop: when the check is met, or the remaining unknowns are cheap and recorded with a default.

Probes: turn something vague into a specific question

Use whenAsk
They talk in abstractions"Walk me through the last time this actually happened. What did you open first?"
The goal is fuzzy"If I handed you the perfect result for that case, what would be on it?"
You need the shape of the data"Can you show me a real one?"
You suspect hidden rules"What do you check by hand before you trust it?"
Use whenAsk
A fact is assumed"Where would I look to find that, and who puts it there?"
A field might be missing"At what moment does that get filled in? Is it ever late, wrong or blank?"
Someone says "usually""Out of ten, how many are right?" (then measure it)
A word is used twice"You said 'account' twice. Is it the same thing both times?"
Something might already exist"Has anyone built something like this here? What happened to it?"
Use whenAsk
A rule sounds absolute"Is there a case where that does not hold?"
You need the quality bar"What would make you stop trusting this? What is the worst wrong answer?"
A step could fail"What if it stops at step three? What state is the user left in?"
A value could be corrected"If they fix that value afterwards, what else should change?"
Experts will want to bypass"Is there a user who already knows this and would skip that step?"
Concurrency or repeats"What if two people do this at once? What if the same request arrives twice?"
Use whenAsk
Everything feels necessary"If this were missing at launch, what could the user not do?"
The plan is large"What is the smallest version someone would actually use on Monday?"
A nice-to-have appears"Can this wait without blocking anything else?"
Use whenAsk
You are stuck on a choice"Whose call is this? Can we get an answer by Friday? What if not?"
It might be reversible"If we choose wrong, how hard is it to undo?" (cheap to reverse: pick and go)
People disagree"What result would make us confident? Can we get it in a day?"
Use whenAsk
Everything seems too smooth"It is six months later and this failed. What is the most likely reason?"
You need to rank risks"Which single step, if wrong, makes the rest worthless?"
Data or systems are unseen"What are we assuming that we have not looked at?"

Good question versus bad question

Bad"What are your non-functional requirements?"
Good"Will two clerks ever work on the same invoice at the same time? If so I'd lock it for whoever opens it first. Does that match how you work?"

Uncertain situations the skill has a move for

Each has a signal, a first move, and a rule for what gets written down so the uncertainty stays visible.

SituationFirst moveIt records
You do not know what you wantShow two or three concrete versions to react to, or walk a recent real caseThe cases; the chosen version as a hypothesis
You want a spec or code right nowDo the ten-minute version of each skipped layer and say the risk that remainsA skip with a reason; the leftover risks
The person who knows is awayWrite the question with a recommended default and proceed on itAn open question with an owner; a tripwire on dependent decisions
No access to real dataMake access the first spike; keep the design cheap to changeFacts marked unknown or inferred; a high-scored risk
Docs and the real system disagreeTrust observation, note the dateA "behaves differently" row and a measured fact
A spec or a build already existsTreat both as hypotheses; reconcile by measurement on fixed inputsA divergence table: spec says, code does, result, choice
Two designs look equally goodFind the assumption that separates them and test it cheaply; if reversible, pick the simplerA decision with alternatives and what would change it
A later layer breaks an earlier oneSay so, run the impact command, reopen the earlier layerA change-log row; bumped versions; updated pins
Scope keeps growingAsk "what breaks if we leave it out?" and cut by thatA deferred list with revisit triggers
The docs are getting unwieldySplit by purpose and link; give each child a reading pathNew small docs; an index that stays accurate
You say "this is too much process"Shrink to the small size; keep the brief, one case, the flow and the planSkipped layers with reasons
The general rule: say what you do not know, say how you would find out and how long that takes, give a default you would proceed with, and write all three down. Uncertainty that is written down is a plan. Uncertainty that stays silent becomes a surprise in week six.
